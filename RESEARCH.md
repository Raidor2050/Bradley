## 3. The two challenged family claims

### 3.1 "Dr Horen Mukherjee — Assam Governor" → DISPROVEN as stated; interpreted as a fusion

- **Documented reality:** No Mookerjee/Mukherjee ever served as Governor of Assam
  (complete official list, colonial 1937–47 and post-1947).
- **The tradition reads as a fusion of two real men:**
  - **H.C. Mookerjee (1887–1956)** — a real "Dr … Mookerjee, Governor", but of
    **WEST BENGAL** (1951–56); Bengali Christian; appropriate to a Christian-family
    tradition.
  - **Syama Prasad Mookerjee (1901–1953)** — the real "Mukherjee + Assam +
    undivided Bengal" figure: Finance Minister of undivided Bengal (1941–42),
    credited with keeping Assam in India (Feb 1947), 1950-riot refugee relief —
    but a minister, **never a governor**, Calcutta/Hooghly roots, not Barisal.
  - Possibly **Hiren Mukerjee** (sound-alike, CPI MP).
- **Verdict:** ADVERSE element stands (no Assam Governor named Mookerjee). Blood
  relationship to any of these men is **unsupported**. Never merge on name
  similarity alone.

### 3.2 "Wells Mission", Shillong (priest: Bashonto Das) → RESOLVED as Welsh Mission; person unverified

- **Round-2 resolution:** "Wells Mission" is phonetically the **Welsh Mission**
  (Presbyterian Church of Wales / Calvinistic Methodist). The **Mawkhar
  Presbyterian Church is its mother church** (mission compound, Mawkhar); the
  first Welsh chapel was built in Shillong in **1874** (Rev. Griffith Hughes);
  the Welsh Mission Hospital operated under Dr H. Gordon Roberts; the **last Welsh
  missionary left in 1969** — precisely matching the anecdotal "Wells Mission
  compound / Pastor Welborn" recollection (c.1965–78).
- **Remaining gap:** no priest named Das (or missionary "Welborn") appears in
  reachable mission records.
- **Verdict:** Mission identification now **supported**; the person (Bashonto Das)
  remains **unverified** pending church baptism/membership registers.

## 4. What the research does confirm (context)

- **Barisal Sadar** localities (Bangla Bazar, Bogura Road, Kazipara, Notun Para)
  are all real places. Barisal is the historic town of the Chandradwip/Bakla
  region; a zamindar-dynasty region; large Hindu out-migration after the 1950,
  1964 and 1971 events (contemporary account: **ICJ 1965 report**, with a
  dedicated Bakarganj/Barisal evidence chapter).
- **Shillong** was the capital of the Chief Commissioner's Province of Assam
  (1874) until 1972; a large Bengali (esp. Sylheti caste-Hindu) settler community
  lived there through the colonial era — consistent with a Das/Mukherjee family
  presence, though not a proof of these specific people.
- The **Mukherjee/Mukhopadhyay lineage** is one of Bengal's great Brahmin
  lineages; village-name+title surnames (Chattopadhyay, Banerjee, Gangopadhyay,
  Mukhopadhyay) are a documented naming system; a published Bengali Brahmin family
  tree even carries a "Mukhuti gram" village node (mukhopadhaya.com).
