# Historical Research — Mukhuti Investigation

The historical era context used to evaluate plausibility of the family timeline.

## Timeline anchors (dataset events, now 15)
- **1797** — Bakerganj district established (→ Barisal).
- **1858** — British Crown takes over East India Company rule.
- **1874** — Shillong made capital of Assam; first Welsh Mission chapel built in
  Shillong (Rev. Griffith Hughes).
- **1887** — H.C. Mookerjee born (context figure).
- **1900s** — Est. era of the "-kanta" grandfather generation in Barisal.
- **1910–1911** — Dwijendra Mukhuti, Sonarang National School headmaster and
  Anushilan Samiti member, convicted in the Sonarang postal peon assault case.
- **1913** — **Barisal Conspiracy Case** (registered 12 May, 44 accused): Hemendra
  Nath Mukhuti (son of Ramanath, Brahmin) discharged; the trial was exercised from
  the Sonarang National School network. The surname's clearest documented tie to
  the Barisal region.
- **1923** — Sudhir Kumar Dass born at Bisharkandi (Banaripara, Barisal district),
  son of Basanti K Mukhuti and Manindra Nath Dass (US SSA record; d. 2007).
- **1947** — Partition; East Pakistan.
- **1950s–1951** — Hindu out-migration from Barisal (ICJ 1965 report documents the
  Bakarganj exodus); H.C. Mookerjee Governor of West Bengal; Syama Prasad Mookerjee
  leads refugee relief.
- **1969** — Last Welsh missionary leaves Shillong (end of the "Wells Mission"
  memory window).
- **1971** — Bangladesh Liberation War.
- **1972** — Shillong ceases to be state capital (Meghalaya created).

## Surname history (verified at surname level)
- Mukhuti/Mukhati = village name in West Bengal + Sanskrit *upadhyaya* (teacher);
  older form of Mukhopadhyay/Mukherjee. Rarhi Kulin Brahmin lineage.
- Alternative hypothesis (Niharranjan Ray, *Bangalir Itihas*): some Mukherjees who
  took up the plough ("langla bamun") adopted Mukhati/Mukhuti; "Mukhoti village
  near Bankura" proposed as a possible origin.
- ~854 "Mukhuti" worldwide (plus ~239 Mukhuty, ~33 Mukhati, ~140 Mukhoti) —
  overwhelmingly West Bengal; a real but rare Brahmin surname.

## Barisal-region presence (Round 2, documented context)
- The surname is now documented in the Barisal-region record of 1910–14 (Barisal
  Conspiracy Case; Sonarang National School) and in a US Social Security record
  from Bisharkandi, Barisal district (1923 birth; 2007 death).
- These are **collateral** findings: same surname and region, no documented blood
  link to the Barisal Sadar family.

## Occupation-era plausibility
- Bengali Brahmin families serving as teachers/priests across colonial Bengal is
  consistent with a priest connection in the family's Shillong story.
- The Welsh Mission's role in colonial Shillong (pioneer chapel 1874, mission
  hospital, education) supports the family's "mission compound" memory.
- A Bengalee engineer in Tata's early industrial era (Moni Das claim) is plausible
  but has no located record.

## What history does NOT provide
- No documented Mukhuti estate in Barisal.
- No priest/SP records for the named Das relatives.
- No public record for any named direct-line relative. The historical record
  enriches context but does not yet extend the verified lineage backward.
